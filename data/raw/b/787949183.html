Roasted Tomatillo Rice

By Paola Briseño-González

Updated September 2, 2026

Media 1 of 1
Ready In
45 min
Rating
4(14)
Comments
Read comments

This fresh take on arroz verde (a Mexican-style green rice pilaf) skips using a blender as in traditional recipes and instead leans on crushed roasted tomatillos, which carry their bright flavor directly into the rice as they simmer together. Green peas add a pop of sweetness, and all of this builds on a comforting pilaf base: rice rinsed first for fluffiness, then cooked with onion and garlic until tender and nutty. A sliced jalapeño gently scents the rice as it simmers. Serve the dish alongside fish, steak or roasted vegetables. Or, for a playful nod to the classic version served at comida corrida lunch spots in Mexico — where a fixed daily menu is the tradition — top it with a sunny-side-up egg, turning this humble rice into a satisfying meal.

Ingredients

Yield:Serves 4 to 6

  • 10 ounces tomatillos (about 5), husks removed and rinsed

  • 1 cup long-grain white rice, such as jasmine or basmati

  • ¼ cup extra-virgin olive oil

  • ½ large yellow onion, finely chopped

  • 2 garlic cloves, finely chopped 

  • 1 cup frozen peas

  • 1¼ cups chicken stock or water

  • Kosher salt, plus more to taste

  • 1 jalapeño, halved lengthwise

  • ⅓ cup fresh parsley leaves, chopped

Preparation

  1. Step 1

    Set oven rack about 6 inches from the broiler and heat broiler to high. Place tomatillos on a rimmed baking sheet and broil until blackened in spots, turning once halfway through cooking, 12 to 14 minutes. Remove from the oven and season with salt.

  2. Step 2

    Meanwhile, in a fine-mesh sieve, rinse the rice with cold water until the water runs almost completely clear. Drain well.

  3. Step 3

    Heat oil in a large stainless steel skillet with a lid over medium-low heat. Add the onion and garlic and cook, stirring constantly, until golden and fragrant, 3 to 5 minutes. Add the rice and stir occasionally until the grains are dry and toasted, about 5 minutes more. Place the broiled tomatillos over the rice and mash them into a pulp with a spatula, folding them into the rice. 

  4. Step 4

    Stir in the peas, stock and 1 teaspoon salt. Top with the halved jalapeño. When the stock comes to a boil, cover the pan and turn heat to low. Cook until the water has been absorbed and the rice is tender, about 15 minutes. Remove pan from heat and let stand, still covered, 10 to 15 minutes more. Uncover and remove the jalapeño with tongs; chop it and stir it back into the rice, or discard if you prefer less spice. Stir in the parsley as you gently fluff rice with a fork. Serve as a side.
